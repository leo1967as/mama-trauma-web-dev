## Day 1–3 — The first days of motherhood

1. **What you might feel:** Joy and excitement can sit beside exhaustion and worry about caring for your baby. Mixed feelings are common and do not mean something is wrong.
2. **Your body:** Hormone levels change quickly after birth. Physical recovery, pain, and fatigue can make emotions feel heavier.
3. **Watch out for:** Seek support for persistent sadness, anxiety, hopelessness, confusion, unusual recurring thoughts, hallucinations, severe agitation, or being unable to sleep even when the baby sleeps.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Rest whenever possible, accept practical help, let yourself cry, and use gentle movement when it feels comfortable.

## Day 4–7 — When emotions may fluctuate most

1. **What you might feel:** Tearfulness, irritability, and worry may peak during this common Baby Blues period and usually ease within the first two weeks.
2. **Your body:** Hormonal change, interrupted sleep, breast discomfort, and birth recovery can heighten fatigue and stress sensitivity.
3. **Watch out for:** Ask for help if you do not want to be near or touch your baby, or anxiety becomes too intense to care for your baby safely.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Allow your feelings, tell someone what you need, drink water, eat something small, stretch gently, and rest when you can.

## Week 2–3 — When people around you return to normal life

1. **What you might feel:** Less help, repeated routines, and broken sleep can bring loneliness, fatigue, and self-doubt.
2. **Your body:** Sleep loss can affect emotions; persistent unusual fatigue or mood changes may also need thyroid assessment.
3. **Watch out for:** Ongoing low mood, loss of pleasure, or thoughts of harming yourself or your baby need prompt support.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Talk with someone you trust, get a little gentle daylight, and remember that asking for help is not weakness.

EPDS review point: Day 8.

## Week 4–6 — A major adjustment period

1. **What you might feel:** Love for your baby can coexist with exhaustion, loneliness, irritability, and wanting time for yourself.
2. **Your body:** Hormones are settling while recovery continues; postpartum hair loss is common and usually improves.
3. **Watch out for:** Intrusive harm thoughts, withdrawing from everyone, or being unable to sleep despite exhaustion warrant support.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Share how you feel at the six-week visit, take short breaks, and allow mixed feelings without self-judgment.

EPDS review point: Day 22.

## Month 2–3 — Transitioning into a new life

1. **What you might feel:** Accumulated fatigue and worries about work and daily balance are understandable during this transition.
2. **Your body:** Thyroid changes and postpartum hair loss may affect energy, mood, and confidence.
3. **Watch out for:** Persistent anger, compulsive checking, hidden depression, guilt, or anxiety that prevents relaxation should be discussed with a professional.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Put your baby somewhere safe if you feel overwhelmed, talk to someone you trust, and remember difficult feelings do not mean you love your baby less.

## Month 4–5 — When your baby changes again

1. **What you might feel:** Sleep and routine changes can bring renewed exhaustion, discouragement, and doubt; these changes are not your fault.
2. **Your body:** Ongoing sleep loss can make emotion regulation and stress harder.
3. **Watch out for:** Persistent hopelessness, numbness, uncontrolled anger, escalating family conflict, or harm thoughts need professional or urgent support.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Ask for a protected block of sleep, tell family when you are depleted, and reserve a short daily recovery activity.

## Month 6 — Another period of change

1. **What you might feel:** New developmental and feeding decisions can bring pressure, comparison, and self-doubt.
2. **Your body:** Reducing breastfeeding may shift oxytocin and prolactin and can unexpectedly affect mood.
3. **Watch out for:** A sharp mood drop after weaning, deep persistent sadness, or anxiety that prevents even brief separation needs support.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Reduce feeds gradually when appropriate, avoid comparing development, discuss post-weaning sadness with a clinician, and be kind to yourself.

## Month 7–9 — When your baby becomes more mobile

1. **What you might feel:** Constant supervision can deepen fatigue and leave less time for yourself.
2. **Your body:** Repeated lifting and carrying may cause back, neck, shoulder, or wrist pain and reduce emotional capacity.
3. **Watch out for:** Depression can begin any time in the first year; persistent sadness, withdrawal, numbness, or harm thoughts need support.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Protect short daily recovery time, share supervision, and tell healthcare staff how you are feeling.

EPDS review point: Day 181.

## Month 10–12 — Approaching the first year

1. **What you might feel:** Pride and joy can coexist with fatigue or memories of pregnancy and birth, especially near the first birthday.
2. **Your body:** Iron, vitamin D, or other nutrient deficiencies may contribute to unusual fatigue or mood changes.
3. **Watch out for:** Persistent depression, distressing birth memories, avoidance, or harm thoughts still need support near the end of the first year.
4. **Tips / Self-care:** Recognize what you have carried, share anniversary-related feelings, and ask a clinician whether nutrient assessment is appropriate.
