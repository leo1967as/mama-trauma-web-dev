# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Primary users are postpartum mothers using Afterbloom on a phone during fragmented, low-energy moments at home, in recovery rooms, or between feeds. They are not browsing for inspiration; they are trying to understand how they feel, complete a gentle daily check-in, notice changes early, and decide what kind of support they may need today.

Secondary users are care-adjacent people who may be referenced through the workflow rather than operating the interface directly: a partner, support circle, or hospital team. The product should help the mother feel safe enough to ask for support without making the experience feel clinical, infantilizing, or surveilled.

## Product Purpose
Afterbloom is a private postpartum support app that turns daily emotional care into a clear, low-friction routine. It combines mood check-ins, emotional screening, support-level guidance, and lightweight care prompts so a mother can understand her current state without needing to interpret raw scores or navigate a complex dashboard.

Success looks like this: the user always knows the next small step, the app spots heavier patterns early, support guidance feels calm rather than alarming, and the product earns enough trust that returning for the next check-in feels easy.

## Brand Personality
Warm, private, steady.

The voice is gentle without becoming cute, calm without becoming vague, and supportive without sounding like a wellness cliché. It should feel like a trusted postpartum companion with credible judgment and emotional restraint.

## Anti-references
Do not make this look like a landing surface or a marketing-first brand site.

Do not make this look like a period tracker.

Do not make this feel childish, sugary, overly sweet, or pastel-toy soft.

Do not use visual or copy patterns that make the user feel judged, monitored, or pushed into urgency before the data supports it.

## Design Principles
1. Reduce emotional load before adding information. The next action must always be obvious, singular, and calm.
2. Translate signals into support, not diagnostics. The product explains what to do next more clearly than why the algorithm decided it.
3. Protect dignity through tone and restraint. Privacy, seriousness, and credibility matter more than delight for its own sake.
4. Make care feel continuous. Home, Mood, EPDS, and support prompts should feel like one coherent care journey, not separate tools.
5. Practice accessible calm. The interface must stay readable, low-friction, and motion-safe when the user is tired, stressed, or using the app one-handed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA across text contrast, focus visibility, touch targets, and error clarity.

Support `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` with non-animated or crossfade alternatives for decorative motion, button effects, and page transitions.

Use emotionally neutral, plain-language copy that works for exhausted users and does not assume high attention or medical literacy.
